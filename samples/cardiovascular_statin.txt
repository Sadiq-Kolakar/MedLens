Background: Statins are recommended for primary prevention in patients with elevated cardiovascular risk, but real-world adherence and outcomes vary across populations.

Methods: This prospective observational cohort study included 3,256 adults aged 40-75 without prior cardiovascular disease who initiated statin therapy between 2018 and 2021 at three urban health systems. Statin adherence was defined as proportion of days covered ≥80% during the first 12 months. The primary outcome was incident major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE) over 24 months.

Results: High adherence was observed in 62.1% of participants. MACE occurred in 4.8% of high-adherence patients versus 9.3% of low-adherence patients (adjusted hazard ratio 0.61; 95% CI 0.49-0.76). LDL cholesterol reduction was greater in the high-adherence group (-38 mg/dL vs -19 mg/dL). The study did not assess diabetes-specific treatment pathways.

Conclusions: Higher statin adherence was associated with lower MACE risk in primary prevention cohorts. Limitations include observational design, residual confounding, and lack of randomized treatment assignment.
